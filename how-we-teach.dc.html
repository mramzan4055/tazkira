How We Teach
Most academies describe their teaching as "interactive" and "engaging" and leave it there. Those are adjectives, not information. Here is what actually happens.
Thirty minutes, minute by minute
Before the class — you need a device with a camera and microphone and a reasonable connection. That is all. Your teacher sends a Zoom link shortly before the session starts.
Minutes 0–3 · Greeting and settling
Salaam, a short exchange, and a moment for a young child to settle. Rushing this costs more time than it saves.
Minutes 3–10 · Revision
Your child recites what was covered last time, starting with something they can already do — which sets the tone as competence rather than correction.
Minutes 10–22 · New material
The teacher reads, your child repeats, the teacher corrects. This is the part an app or recording cannot replicate: a person listening to your specific child.
Minutes 22–27 · Practice and review
Interactive quizzes and short games that turn recall into something a child wants to win rather than something they are being tested on.
Minutes 27–30 · Setting the week
A clear, specific practice task — not "revise this" but a defined portion, and how long it should take.
After the class
The teacher records what was covered, what went well and what needs attention. That record becomes your monthly report.
Why we do not ask a seven-year-old to concentrate like an adult
A child of six or seven can hold sustained attention for roughly the length of their age in minutes. Asking for forty-five is not discipline — it is a design error.
So sessions are thirty minutes, split into short blocks, with the type of activity changing every few minutes. Recall is built in as a game rather than added as a test. And the session always opens with something the child can already do.
The interactive element — we use quiz-style review for recall — short, competitive, and something children ask to do again. It is the single most common thing parents mention after a first class.
And when it still does not work — sometimes a child and a teacher simply do not click. That is not a failure of either. Tell us, and we will change teacher without asking you to justify it.
How you will know if it is working
Every session is recorded against a simple structure: what was covered, what was secure, what needs work. Those notes become a monthly report written in plain English. A monthly report tells you:
✓What your child covered this month.
✓What improved, specifically — which letters, which rules, which portions.
✓What is still difficult and what the teacher is doing about it.
✓What comes next, and roughly when.
What happens if your child falls behind
It happens, and it is usually one of four things: the pace is wrong, the foundations underneath are weaker than they appeared, the timing of the class is bad, or the teacher fit is off.
What we do is stop and diagnose rather than push forward. Pushing forward on shaky foundations produces a child who reads confidently and incorrectly, which takes far longer to undo.
Your teacher will tell you when this is happening. You should not have to work it out from your child's mood.
